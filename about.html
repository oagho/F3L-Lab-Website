About the F3L

Our Mission

The Fatigue, Fracture, and Failure Laboratory (F3L) at the University of Toledo is dedicated to uncovering the fundamental mechanisms governing the structural integrity and durability of advanced engineering materials. Our work bridges the gap between fundamental materials science and mission-critical engineering applications.

We collaborate closely with industrial partners and federal agencies—including the Department of Defense and NASA—to deliver scalable mechanical testing solutions that optimize advanced alloy performance and shorten testing lifecycles for extreme service environments.

Core Competencies & Methodologies

F3L utilizes advanced diagnostics and mechanical stress equipment to analyze deformation, damage accumulation, and failure under complex service conditions:

High-Throughput Fatigue Life

Accelerating fatigue life quantification using high-speed automated loading systems to test materials across extensive cyclic regimes efficiently.

Very High Cycle Fatigue (VHCF)

Evaluating lightweight structural alloys beyond 107 cycles up to 109 cycles to detect subsurface crack initiation and internal flaw dynamics.

Additive Manufacturing Integrity

Investigating the process–structure–property relationships, defect tolerance, and fatigue behavior of laser powder bed fusion and wire arc additively manufactured metals.

Extreme Environment Assessment

Characterizing material response across severe thermal fluctuations, high-temperature creep-fatigue conditions, and aggressive corrosive environments.
