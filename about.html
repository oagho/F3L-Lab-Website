About the F3L Lab

Our Mission

The Failure, Fracture, and Fatigue Laboratory (F3L) at the University of Toledo is dedicated to unlocking the fundamental mechanisms governing the structural integrity and longevity of novel engineering materials. Our work sits at the intersection of practical engineering application and deep material science exploration.

We work tightly alongside industrial stakeholders and federal sponsors—including the Department of Defense and NASA—to deliver scalable mechanical testing solutions that optimize advanced alloy performance and speed up testing cycles for extreme aerospace environments.

History

F3L utilizes state-of-the-art diagnostic and mechanical stress equipment to break down physical anomalies under varied environmental settings. Our core research competencies include:

High-Throughput Fatigue Life

Accelerating traditional fatigue quantification schemas down to manageable lifecycles via custom, high-speed automated loading paradigms.

Very High Cycle Fatigue (VHCF)

Subjecting lightweight structural alloys to structural limits beyond 107 cycles to uncover sub-surface flaw configurations and micro-cracking behavior.

Severe Environment Assessment

Evaluating material response matrices across rapid structural thermal fluctuations, elevated system heats, and extreme atmosphere exposure.
